Behaviour lives in `src/features/raukk_sourcing/calculations/shippingDamage.ts` and `shippingDamage.types.ts` over the assets `raukk_stellar.json`, `raukk_pressure.json` and `raukk_orbits.json`; their JSDoc and `src/locales/en_US/raukk_sourcing.json` are authoritative for how it behaves, `facts/star-heat-damage.json` for every constant, equation and measurement, and the transcripts `btf_flights.json`, `btf_star_damage.json`, `btf_ant_reflight.json`, `star_damage_community.json`, `repair_and_damage.json`, `ki439_orbit_log.json` and `station_commissioning.json` for the flights, the shield table and the universe start date. ## Defect: heat and radiation are merged (§7.5)

`shippingDamage.ts:131-145` carries the defect itself — one `stellar` type where the game has two, and `reliefOf` summing a heat relief onto a radiation one and capping at 1. What the code cannot carry is the evidence, the symmetry of the failure, and the verdict.

- The proof is `damageModifiers` and `damageTypeSplit` in `repair_and_damage.json`, and the split there is MEASURED, not inferred: fly a lane bare in the BTF simulator, re-fly with one shield type installed, attribute the drop, wear is the remainder. Our own panels print one damage figure per leg and can never see it, which is why merging looked free. Greps `damageTypeSplit`, `damageModifiers`, `differencing`, `not measurable` all miss.
- The failure runs BOTH ways and neither direction is small: a heat shield can zero a lane that is almost pure radiation, a radiation shield can gut a lane that is almost pure heat. Per-lane composition and both consequences are in FACTS. Greps `Giedi`, `Ice Station` miss — no lane composition reaches `src`.
- Verdict: bare hulls are trustworthy, shield comparisons are not. Treat any output that varies a stellar shield fit as unsourced until the term is split. Grep `shield fit` misses.
- It is the one defect a planning horizon makes worse rather than better. Everything else in the residual is a phase draw that averages out over a plan's months; this is a fixed mispricing for as long as the shields stay fitted. `shippingDamage.ts:30-37` argues the averaging case for the model as a whole and does not carve this out.

## Rejected

- **The orbit-implied luminosity ladder** (O = 32x G, from the median innermost orbit). It assumed the generator places the innermost planet at constant flux; it does not. Real spread is O/M ~ 750,000 — class table in `star_damage_community.json`. Greps `luminosity ladder`, `orbit-implied`, `constant flux` miss; bare `ladder` hits only `shippingDamage.test.ts:721`, the NL-534 distance-collapse test, which is a different ladder.
- **Splitting the stellar coefficient by leg type.** The DEP/APP split is real (1.5x, holding across a ship change) but tuning two constants makes the BOUNDS worse — worst escape multiplied by an order of magnitude, figures in FACTS — because the per-epoch solves are exactly determined and their coefficient absorbs whatever the geometry gets wrong. Greps `depCoefficient`, `appCoefficient`, `legTypeCoefficient`, `28.9%` miss.
- **Refitting the stellar coefficient at all.** A global refit and a DEP/APP pair both zero the pooled bias and both leave the per-anchor spread exactly where it was. That is the signature of a residual that is not a scaling error, so no scalar can fix it. Greps `2.956`, `refit` miss in the damage model.
- **Lane inclination as the explanation of the per-anchor residuals.** `rho`, a lane's in-plane component, is not a free parameter: `fio_systemstars.json` gives the lane vector and each system's `Connections` gives the FTL edge a leg actually flies, and because the warp point is set by the NEXT hop rather than the final destination, a multi-jump route's DEP and APP legs point somewhere other than where they end up. Computing it properly barely moves the pooled bias (figures in FACTS) and leaves every large per-anchor residual untouched, because those anchors are all NARROW-band ones that tilt cannot reach. Tilt is therefore ruled OUT, and section 9.2's open item is worth less than it looked; the remaining candidates are a per-anchor coefficient, a bad `Sunlight`, or single-capture phase. Greps `rules out`, `next hop`, `jump sequence`, `Connections` (in the damage model) miss; `rho` appears only in `shippingDamage.test.ts` as a sensitivity check that never prices a real leg.
- **The community landing formula** (`star_damage_community.json`, `landingDamageCandidate`). Shape kept, coefficient refitted: theirs runs uniformly ~1.9x high on this campaign's landings. Grep `1.9x` misses.
- **Reading the sim clock as time since the servers came up.** That demands a 47x game/real ratio, which the 74-flight fit rejects (r = 0.06 against 0.974). Greps `servers came up`, `47x` miss.
- **Identifying the universe start date with orbital zero.** The start date itself is established, not rejected — see the next section — but setting `MEAN_ANOMALY_AT_EPOCH = 0` at that instant is a separate claim and it does not survive. Two ways to try it, both failing: hold the fitted 20x ratio and an orbital zero 2,000 real days back scores r = -0.972 on the 74-flight log; hold instead the orbital clock's present reading of 258.4 game years and the ratio must become 47.19x, which is the servers-came-up reading above (r = 0.073) and which also shrinks the KI-439b/d synodic period from 5.72 to 2.42 real days against a series measuring 5.71. Greps `47.19`, `475.512` miss.

## The universe start date (established), and whether the map was at zero (open)

**The universe simulation started 2,000 real days ago, give or take a day.** This is a game-stated figure, not a fit: all four founding exchanges — ANT, BEN, MOR, HRT — print `Commissioned: 2,000 days ago` in their STNS panels, so the number dates the universe and not any one station. Transcribed with the two later stations in `station_commissioning.json`; those read 1,643 days, and it is the existence of a second distinct tier that says the field is a real timestamp rather than a placeholder. The panel prints whole days and carries no hour, which is why the tolerance is a day and not tighter. In game time that is 109.51 game years at the 20x ratio. Nothing in `src` consumes this yet.

**Settled: the map was NOT at zero on that day.** `MEAN_ANOMALY_AT_EPOCH = 0` (`shippingDamage.ts:790`) says every body sits at periapsis simultaneously at the orbital epoch, so asking whether the generator started the universe from that configuration is asking whether universe start and orbital zero are the same instant. They are not, and the gap is now a measured number rather than an aliased one: **orbital zero is 2013-09-13T21:45Z, which is 2,719.72 real days — 148.92 game years — BEFORE universe commissioning.** The generator seeded the bodies mid-orbit. At commissioning the four inner KI-439 bodies span 295 degrees of true longitude, where a zero start would put all four at 0.

The evidence is `system_map_positions.json`: three system maps captured in one sitting, twelve bodies, angles read off the images by eye. Each system's absolute rotation is free, so the data is nine relative angles against one unknown — the capture instant. That fit lands at 2026-08-16T15:04Z with an RMS of 5.8 degrees — reading error plus the accumulated phase error costed two subsections down, not reading error alone — and it is the ONLY minimum under 25 degrees across the 2,160 days scanned from before commissioning through today. Three systems whose synodic periods are mutually incommensurate share no other common solution over the universe's whole lifetime, so the 5.7182-day lattice described in the next section is broken and the epoch is pinned absolutely.

Two things fell out of that fit and both matter more than the question it was run to answer:

- **The common-epoch claim is real, and had never actually been tested.** `MEAN_ANOMALY_AT_EPOCH = 0` for every body is what makes twelve bodies fit one parameter to reading error, and it would have failed loudly otherwise: the same fit run without the map's chirality correction sits at 53 degrees, and uniform noise scores 104. The 74-flight log could not have tested it. One pair yields exactly one observable, the relative angle, and a per-planet phase offset enters it only as `phi_b - phi_d`, which any epoch shift of `(phi_b - phi_d) x synodic / 2pi` absorbs exactly. One constraint, one parameter, residual driven to zero for ANY true offsets — `shippingDamage.ts:786` calls that verification and it is the one claim in this doc that was circular. What the 74 flights do establish stands: the Kepler periods, the 20x ratio, and the synodic period.
- **The map frame is a REFLECTION of the model's perifocal frame**, equivalently the model advances every body in the sense opposite to the map. Consistent across all three systems, so it is a rendering convention rather than a per-system quirk. Reflection preserves distance, so no separation, damage figure or shipping distance moves and there is nothing to fix in `src`; it matters only when comparing model output against map imagery, which is exactly what the next person to try this will do.

The fit was given no date. It searched 2,160 days, returned one instant, and that instant fell 2.4 hours from the real session clock — landing on the correct day out of 2,160 candidates is the confirmation, and it is what licenses everything above. The capture instant is nonetheless SOLVED rather than observed, and the fit only degrades to 7.0 degrees RMS at plus or minus an hour, so the hour itself stays soft.

A ruler pass over the same maps, given as ahead/behind relations, corroborates the reflected frame on signs alone — no angle precision involved. It was then read as bracketing the capture instant to 51 minutes, by taking the model's alignment crossings (KI-439a passes KI-439d at 15:50Z, ZV-759a passes Agamemnon at 16:41Z) as exact clock edges. **That bracket was wrong and is withdrawn.** A crossing time is only as sharp as the model's phase, and the model's phase is not sharp at all — KI-439a's carries roughly 23 degrees, which at 5.6 degrees an hour is plus or minus four hours on the crossing. The near-alignment trick is real but it cuts the other way: a pair sitting within the model's own error of alignment is where the predicted sign is LEAST trustworthy, not most.

### The phase error nobody had costed

The model's positions today carry **5 to 20 degrees of unavoidable error**, and it is the dominant term — larger than reading angles off a screenshot by eye. The cause is leverage: the epoch is 4,720 real days back, which is 350 revolutions for KI-439d and 2,135 for KI-439a, so any relative error in a period is multiplied by the revolution count before it reaches today's phase. Periods come from Kepler on `raukk_orbits.json`'s semi-major axes, stored to five significant figures, and on FIO star masses of unstated precision. A single part in 10^5 on KI-439a's period is 7.7 degrees of phase now; the stored axis's half-ulp alone is 23 degrees.

Live observation confirms this is what the residual is, rather than anything structural:

| pair | revolutions since epoch | budget from 5-sig-fig `a` | observed miss |
|---|---|---|---|
| VH-331 c/e | 680 | 3.6 deg | 2.0 deg |
| KI-439 b/c | 1,186 | 9.6 deg | 8.6 deg |
| ZV-759 a/d | 1,659 | 11.2 deg | 7.5 deg |
| KI-439 a/d | 2,135 | 23.4 deg | 7.0 deg |

Monotone in the revolution count, and the slowest pair matches best. A wrong epoch or a wrong frame does not produce that pattern. The epoch is untouched: the fit scores 5.8 degrees and its nearest rival across 2,160 days scores 36.0, so ten or twenty degrees of drift cannot reach another minimum.

The fix is cheap and is now the one thing worth building here. A single timestamped map per system yields every body's phase correction directly — `offset = observed - trueAnomaly(t)` with the reflection applied — and that correction absorbs the whole accumulated period error at once, taking in-system positions from 10-degree accuracy to observation accuracy. `Shiptronia` resolved to VH-331e, which is also what made the VH-331 c/e row above possible, and it independently confirms the VH-331 ordering that the radius ratios had left as the weakest identification in the set.

## Constraints the code obeys but cannot explain

- **Every orbital plane is assumed to be the galactic plane.** No source states it and none carries an inclination at all: `raukk_orbits.json` holds `[semiMajorAxis, eccentricity]` and nothing more, and both the community visualiser and `raukkPlanetPosition` model orbits flat and coplanar. It is the assumption that makes `rho` computable without fitting — and since computing `rho` buys nothing per anchor, it is not load-bearing either way today. Greps `coplanar`, `OrbitInclination`, `RightAscension`, `orbitalPlane` miss; `inclination` hits only `shippingDamage.ts:26`, which asserts that inclination does not average away without ever saying which frame it is measured in.
- `RAUKK_DAMAGE_SIM_CALIBRATION_MS` and `..._YEARS` are copied off a third-party page but not carried on trust: sweeping either destroys the 74-flight fit — half a game year, or one percent on the ratio, flips r from 0.974 to negative. Grep `third-party` misses across `src/features/raukk_sourcing/`; the only trace in the model is the bare URL at `shippingDamage.ts:109`.
- The epoch aliases — 200.0 game years also scores 0.970, 115.0 scores -0.970 — because a 21-day window cannot separate epochs offset by a common multiple of both periods. This was a real limit of the FLIGHT LOG and it is now retired: the three-system map fit of the section above has one solution across the universe's lifetime. Kept because the aliasing itself is still the reason a single pair can never pin an epoch, which is the trap to avoid next time. Grep `alias` misses.
- The alias PERIOD, when a single pair is all you have, is that pair's synodic period — 5.7182 real days for KI-439b/d, not the 31 game years the two named aliases happen to sit apart (566.14 real days, which is 99.0 synodic periods — an alias because of the 99, not because of the 31). What holds the lattice open is that both orbits are near-circular (e = 0.0193 and 0.0180), so a separation is a function of the RELATIVE angle to within about 2%, below the log's noise; the absolute phases each body would need for eccentricity to break the tie are not separately observable in a separation series. Greps `synodic`, `5.7182` miss in `src`; `raukkSynodicPeriodDays` computes the quantity at `shippingDamage.ts:767` and no caller feeds it an epoch.
- What a separation series fixes is PHASE, not age, and only the phase of the ONE pair it watches. Breaking out of that needs bodies whose synodic periods do not share a common multiple, which in practice means several systems observed at one instant — a system map does it in a single screenshot and 74 flights over 21 days do not. `RAUKK_DAMAGE_SIM_CALIBRATION_YEARS` took its step from the community visualiser on trust; the map fit has since confirmed that step, so the constant is now independently sourced rather than borrowed.
- Capture dates. Panels within ONE batch were minutes apart, so a planet holds one orbital position per batch; ACROSS batches the dates are NOT established. The 6.3 and 7.5 real-hour gaps in FACTS are inferred from the fit, not known. Greps `capturedAt`, `captureTime`, `capture date` miss; the only mention of a batch anywhere in the model is the prose at `shippingDamage.test.ts:248`, which carries no date either.

## Unbuilt

- **Nothing consumes the model.** `shippingDamage` appears nowhere in `src` outside its own two files and its test, nor do `raukkTripDamage`, `raukkLegDamage` or `raukkStellarGeometry`, and `src/locales/en_US/raukk_sourcing.json` carries no key for it. The wired wear model is still `shippingPhysics.ts:547` (`raukkStlDamage`, wear + meteoroid only), so no view prices a stellar term.
- **Splitting the stellar term in two.** The fix for §7.5 is a second coefficient and a second relief table, not a constant — the sheet's per-lane heat share spans the whole 0 to 1, so no ratio collapses it. Blocked on nothing but the work: `repair_and_damage.json` already carries both the modifiers and the measured split.
- **Repeat flights on ONE lane, spread across its orbital period.** The only way to separate a genuine per-lane bias from a phase draw, and the only route to per-lane accuracy that does not first need section 9.2's plane orientation. Cost is one BTF panel per capture, about five captures across the anchor's period — days for a tight orbit, months for a wide one. Worth doing for a lane a base depends on, never universally. Greps `five captures`, `hide under` miss.
- **The warp-IN asymmetry.** Departures out of one system all measure the same distance whatever planet they leave from; approaches on what resolves to the same inbound lane vary 21.0 to 27.4 Mkm and leave a 14% residual on the position fit. The model treats DEP and APP alike. (`asymmetr` hits only `shippingPhysics.ts:204`, the DEP/APP FUEL share.)
- **Solving each planet's position rather than bounding it.** `fio_systemstars.json` carries `PositionX/Y/Z` per system, so the direction to any target is computable and only one angle per planet per day is unknown. Three lanes off one anchor fit it exactly-determined — consistency, not proof; it wants five or more in one sitting. Greps `over-determin`, `exactly determined` miss.
- **Orbital-plane orientation in the galactic frame** — two fixed angles per system, published by no source. Fit them once and that system's stellar term becomes a point estimate instead of a band. Blocked on flights carrying no capture time, and demoted by §7.3: solving it would not move the per-anchor residuals it was expected to explain.
- **One blueprint flown both directions on a single pair**, settling the DEP/APP split by direct measurement rather than cross-batch inference.
- **Re-capturing the three system maps with the instant recorded to the minute.** The one outstanding check on the map fit, and the cheapest item in this doc: the 2026-08-16T15:04Z capture instant is solved rather than observed, and a real timestamp either confirms it or refutes the epoch reconstruction outright. Screenshots read by eye are good enough — the residual is already only reading error.
- **Per-body phase corrections.** Promoted from fallback to the one build worth doing here, because the phase error above is measured rather than hypothetical: the model is 5 to 20 degrees out today and cannot improve while its periods come from five-significant-figure axes. One timestamped map per system fixes every body in it at once, `offset = observed - trueAnomaly(t)`, absorbing the accumulated period error whatever its source. Two captures a few days apart would additionally separate a constant offset from a residual period error, which is the difference between a correction that holds for months and one that decays.
